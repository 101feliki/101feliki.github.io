At Birdview Microinsurance

Four systems for a microinsurer's daily operations

As a Software Engineering Intern (Nov 2025 – Jan 2026) I developed backend services and automation tools across the business.

  • Facial recognition attendance: staff check in and out by face, with identity verified by MTCNN and SSD MobileNet models. It automated attendance for 50+ staff, with APIs for logging, authentication, reporting and analytics dashboards.
  • Financial transaction management: validated database updates and audit-trail logic across 100+ transaction entries, improving data consistency for internal workflows.
  • Customer notification platform: multi-channel SMS and email alerts that cut manual communication delays with customers.
  • Asset registry: lifecycle management that digitised tracking for 200+ organisational assets.

The value: less time on registers, spreadsheets and phone calls, and records an auditor can trust: who attended, what was paid, what the company owns, and what each customer was told.

01

Microinsurance Fraud Detection

A plug-and-play service that scores each claim for fraud risk, explains why a claim was flagged, and gives analysts a dashboard to decide. It can sit in front of any policy or claims system.

Claims system→Claim sent for scoring→Express API→FastAPI scoring→Score 0–1 + reasons→Analyst dashboard
  • The API adds context (the client's claims in the last 30 days) before scoring.
  • Signals include amounts far above normal for the product, several claims in a short time, claims right after a policy starts, late reporting and suspiciously round amounts.
  • Low-risk claims are approved automatically; claims scoring 0.6 or more go to an analyst, who sees plain reasons like "Claim made 12 days after the policy started".
  • Scores are deterministic: the same claim always gets the same score, so every decision can be audited.

02

InsureLog

A logging toolkit for insurance microservices. Add one dependency to any Spring Boot service and it gets structured insurance events, automatic personal-data masking, and correlation IDs that follow a customer from policy to claim to payment.

policy-serviceclaims-servicepayments-service→batched async HTTP→insurelog-server→Prometheus→Grafana
  • Core library: the event model, personal-data masking, validation, shipping to the server and metrics, usable without any framework.
  • Spring Boot starter: the plug-and-play part. Add it to a service and everything is set up automatically.
  • Central server: collects events from every service, with search, "trace a customer journey", CSV and JSON export, and API docs.
  • A demo claims service generates live traffic to show it working end to end.

Why it matters: when a customer says "my claim disappeared", support can follow that customer's correlation ID across every service, without anyone seeing their ID number or phone in the logs.

03

Policy follow-up mailer

A command-line tool that reads a list of clients from Excel or CSV and sends each one a personalised follow-up email, for example to customers who started buying an insurance product but didn't finish.

  • Recognises common column names (Name / Client Name, Email / Email Address, Product).
  • Skips rows with missing names, invalid emails or duplicates, and reports exactly which rows and why.
  • HTML and plain-text emails with client data safely escaped; company details, logo and products come from settings, nothing hard-coded.
  • A delay between sends to stay within mail-provider limits.

How they fit together

Together they cover a customer's whole journey: the mailer brings back customers who dropped off, InsureLog traces them across every service once they're insured, and fraud detection protects the claims they make. Each one stands alone, so an insurer can adopt one without the others.
